90 Days Ahead

The clock doesn’t stop. The tasks don’t stop. Your daily assignments don’t stop. This is how you carry all of it — without losing your mind or your responsibilities.

The System in Five Steps

  1. Get a calendar. Paper, phone, wall — it does not matter what it looks like or how it functions. What matters: one place where your whole life is visible.
  2. Start personal. Lay out your big personal dates first — Momma’s birthday, your spouse’s birthday, Christmas. Personal is the easiest thing to schedule; it builds the muscle.
  3. Count back 90 calendar days (not business days) from every major event and mark the ladder: 90 – 60 – 30 – 14.
  4. Systematize your work. For every big deliverable, write out every step, then walk backwards from the due date giving each step its own deadline. Some chains need 180 days — map it all the way out.
  5. Walk the calendar daily. Reset monthly. Ninety seconds every morning: “Is anything due for a 90/60/30/14 today?” Top of every month: reset, keep going.

The 6 Ways the System Breaks

  1. Systems not aligned — the plan doesn’t match how work really flows
  2. Time estimates wrong — 5 days scheduled for a 15-day task
  3. Messy folders — right plan, can’t find anything
  4. Wrong questions — planned on assumptions, not information
  5. Not the sign-off — approval lives on someone else’s desk; schedule their time
  6. No buffer — the plan only works if nothing goes wrong

Part 3 — Partner Stress-Test

  • Where is the buffer? (If every estimate must be perfect, the plan is already broken.)
  • Who actually signs off — and is their time on this map?
  • Which estimate are you least sure of? Double it and see what breaks.
  • What information don’t you have yet — and who has it?
  • Has your 90-day start date already passed? What’s the recovery move this week?

Part 4 — The Daily Habit

  • Every morning (90 seconds): open the calendar — “Is anything due for a 90/60/30/14 today?”
  • Every reminder gets an alert — day before, hour before, 5 minutes before. A reminder that doesn’t fire is a diary entry.
  • Clean folders: when a milestone fires, find the file in seconds — not an hour of digging. (Let Claude organize your desktop and folders if you need a reset.)
  • Top of every month: re-walk everything. Reset, keep going. Reset, keep going.
